## Data cleaning log
- Exact duplicate rows: 0 (each row is a distinct book-year list slot, so nothing to drop)
- Unified 2 author name variants affecting 9 rows ('George R.R. Martin' -> 'George R. R. Martin', 'J.K. Rowling' -> 'J. K. Rowling')

- 3 title-year pairs appear twice (different editions of the same book in one year); kept as separate list slots
- NOTE: `reviews` (and mostly `rating`) is a single 2020 snapshot repeated for every year a book appears; it is NOT a per-year measure, so review trends over time are not analysed

## 1. Non-fiction dominates, and the mix is stable
- Non-fiction holds **56%** of all list slots.
- Fiction peaked at 58% in 2014 (the only fiction-majority year) and bottomed at 34% in 2015.
- No significant trend in the fiction share over the decade (slope -0.33 pp/year, p = 0.624).

## 2. Ratings are high, compressed, and rising
- **80%** of books are rated 4.5 or higher, so ratings barely discriminate between bestsellers.
- Fiction books have a slightly higher median rating (4.7 vs 4.6; Mann-Whitney p = 0.017, rank-biserial -0.15, a small effect) but also the lowest-rated outliers.
- The mean rating of list books rose from **4.58 (2009) to 4.74 (2019)** (Spearman ρ = 0.29, p < 0.001). Caveat: ratings are a 2020 snapshot, so this reflects *which* books made the list, not rating changes over time.

## 3. Books got cheaper
- Median paid price fell from **$12 to $10** (Spearman ρ = -0.22, p < 0.001).
- Non-fiction is slightly pricier (median $12 vs $11), but the difference is not significant at the 5% level (p = 0.077).
- 12 list slots were listed at $0 and are excluded from price statistics. The priciest titles are reference works and box sets (e.g. the DSM-5 at $105).

## 4. Fiction drives engagement
- Fiction books collect far more reviews (median 8,840 vs 5,041; p < 0.001, rank-biserial -0.36).
- Review count correlates with longevity on the list (ρ = 0.40) but hardly with rating (ρ = 0.11).

## 5. A long tail of authors, with a few franchises
- 246 authors; **53%** appear exactly once, and the top 10 hold 17% of all slots.
- **Jeff Kinney** leads with 12 appearances from 12 different titles (a new *Diary of a Wimpy Kid* book in every single year), while **Gary Chapman** earns 11 appearances from just 2 titles.

## 6. Most bestsellers are one-year wonders
- **73%** of books appear in only one year.
- The longest-running titles, *The 5 Love Languages: The Secret to Love That Lasts* (Gary Chapman) and *Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, 6th Edition* (American Psychological Association), appeared in 10 of 11 years. The evergreens are dominated by self-help, reference and children's books.

## Limitations
- Top-50 rank within a year is not available, so all list slots are weighted equally.
- `reviews` and `rating` are a single snapshot (2020) repeated across years.
- Genre is a coarse two-way split; no sub-genres or page counts.
